# Evaluation Methodology

## Goal

Evaluate whether longitudinal software-quality digital twins provide useful information beyond static software-quality checks, and determine the conditions under which history improves or harms assurance decisions.

The evaluation separates five properties that should not be collapsed into one score:

1. **current-state correctness** — does the twin represent present quality accurately?
2. **trajectory sensitivity** — does it recognize meaningful degradation or recovery across versions?
3. **forecast usefulness** — does history provide actionable warning before an absolute threshold is crossed?
4. **fidelity and robustness** — does the twin remain stable under noise and respond appropriately to real quality change?
5. **human interpretability** — can reviewers verify why a state or warning was produced?

## Primary Comparisons

The core experimental conditions are:

1. **Static threshold-based SQA** — current telemetry only, no history.
2. **Current-version twin state** — the digital-twin state estimator without longitudinal trend.
3. **Longitudinal twin** — current state plus version-history and trend features.
4. **Longitudinal twin + evidence-linked explanation** — same model with evidence and trend rationale shown to reviewers.

The first three conditions enable technical comparison. The fourth supports later human-centered evaluation.

## Ground Truth

Every benchmark trajectory must define expected quality states independently of model execution.

Ground truth should include:

- expected risk level for every version;
- whether the version is part of a meaningful degradation trajectory;
- whether a warning would be considered useful before the next release;
- whether the trajectory represents recovery, stability, or relapse;
- the rationale used to assign each label.

Expected labels must not be generated by the same scoring function being evaluated.

## Scenario Families

The benchmark should contain multiple independent trajectories from the following families:

- stable healthy evolution;
- gradual coverage erosion;
- complexity accumulation;
- defect growth;
- test-change misalignment;
- sudden regression;
- compound gradual degradation;
- recovery after remediation;
- partial recovery and relapse;
- noisy telemetry;
- missing or delayed telemetry;
- large but well-tested benign changes.

A single trajectory is insufficient because it cannot establish general behavior across different forms of software-quality evolution.

## Metrics

### Quality-state performance

- exact risk-classification accuracy;
- macro F1 across low/medium/high risk;
- mean ordinal risk error;
- unsafe undercall rate;
- confusion matrix.

Ordinal error is important because predicting `low` when the expected state is `high` is more serious than predicting `medium` when the expected state is `high`.

### Drift performance

- true-positive degradation detection;
- false-positive drift alerts;
- detection lead time;
- magnitude error for score change;
- recovery detection delay.

### Early-warning performance

- early-warning rate before an increase in ground-truth risk;
- false-warning rate when future risk does not increase;
- warning lead time in versions;
- proportion of warnings that lead to useful human review.

### Forecasting performance

- release recommendation accuracy;
- conservative versus unsafe forecast errors;
- calibration when probabilistic forecasts are introduced;
- projected-score error for one-step forecasts;
- trajectory-family-specific performance.

### Twin fidelity

- responsiveness to controlled quality changes;
- stability under unchanged quality conditions;
- robustness under missing or noisy telemetry;
- agreement with independently defined ground truth;
- monotonic response to controlled degradation severity;
- recovery responsiveness after remediation.

### Human-centered outcomes

For reviewer studies:

- verification accuracy;
- time to decision;
- perceived explanation usefulness;
- appropriate reliance on the forecast;
- correction rate when the twin is wrong;
- review workload.

## Longitudinal Trend Evaluation

The implemented trend model uses a rolling window over versioned quality states and records:

- mean version-to-version score delta;
- cumulative score delta;
- consecutive decline count;
- degradation flag;
- recovery flag.

Trend thresholds are research parameters rather than universal quality standards. They must be tuned on development trajectories and frozen before final evaluation.

The central history-ablation comparison is:

```text
current-state twin
vs.
current-state twin + trend history
```

History is only valuable if it improves at least one meaningful outcome, such as earlier degradation warning or fewer unsafe undercalls, without creating unacceptable false-warning burden.

## Baseline Design

The repository includes a transparent current-version threshold baseline using coverage, complexity, failing tests, defect counts, change size, and test-change alignment.

A useful baseline should be credible enough that outperforming it is meaningful. Weak or intentionally simplistic baselines should not be used to inflate apparent gains from the digital twin.

Future baselines should include:

- moving-average quality rules;
- conventional statistical process control;
- logistic or ordinal regression over telemetry;
- tree-based tabular models;
- history-free machine-learning baselines;
- history-aware statistical forecasting where dataset size permits.

## Ablation Studies

Remove one information source at a time to measure its contribution:

- version history;
- coverage;
- complexity;
- failing tests;
- defect counts;
- change size;
- test-change alignment.

Also vary:

- trend window length;
- drift threshold;
- quality-score weights;
- warning threshold;
- missingness rate;
- telemetry noise magnitude.

## Robustness Experiments

### Missing telemetry

Randomly and systematically remove quality signals to measure how rapidly fidelity and forecast performance degrade.

### Noisy telemetry

Inject bounded measurement error into coverage, complexity, defects, or change size and measure state stability.

### Delayed telemetry

Withhold defect or test information until later versions to simulate real CI/CD reporting delays.

### Threshold sensitivity

Repeat experiments across reasonable threshold ranges to determine whether conclusions depend on one hand-picked configuration.

## Statistical Analysis

Once multiple independent trajectories are available:

- report confidence intervals for primary metrics;
- use paired comparisons across methods evaluated on the same version points;
- report effect sizes in addition to p-values;
- separate development/tuning trajectories from held-out evaluation trajectories;
- report per-family results rather than only pooled averages;
- correct for repeated comparisons when many variants are tested.

Where trajectory points are correlated, avoid treating every version as an independent sample without accounting for within-trajectory dependence.

## Reproducibility Requirements

Every experiment should publish or record:

- dataset and trajectory version;
- telemetry definitions and units;
- quality-state formula or model version;
- all thresholds and trend-window settings;
- baseline definitions;
- random seeds where applicable;
- raw states, forecasts, warnings, and explanations;
- evaluation metrics;
- excluded cases and reasons;
- negative and contradictory results.

## Validity Safeguards

The current synthetic benchmark and hand-designed state estimator share some assumptions. This can inflate apparent performance.

To reduce circularity:

- construct held-out trajectories independently;
- freeze scoring and threshold parameters before evaluation;
- introduce externally defined quality histories;
- compare with non-twin statistical baselines;
- perform sensitivity analysis over scoring weights;
- report cases where longitudinal context makes decisions worse.
